Classroom Wi-Fi CSI | Field notes for Rooms 1-4 | 25 November 2026

What the team collected
This folder contains 31 separate 30-second recordings from four classrooms:
7 in Room 1 and 8 each in Rooms 2, 3 and 4. The question is whether a Wi-Fi
link in the corridor changes when one person sits, stands or walks inside
the classroom. "Empty" always refers to the classroom. Corridor activity is
a separate condition. A recording measures the radio link; it does not by
itself establish occupancy or which path the signal took into the room.

Hardware and placement
- TX and RX are two 7Semi ESP32-S3 boards with built-in PCB antennas. TX
  sends ESP-NOW packets on Wi-Fi channel 6 at a target rate of 50 per second.
  RX records channel state information (CSI) from the selected transmitter.
- The shared setup called for both boards outside on the same corridor side:
  TX at the classroom door and RX along the adjoining wall, 3 m apart
  horizontally, center to center. The instructions specified a closed door,
  upright boards with antenna ends up, and a 5 cm gap from each surface.
  Room 1 explicitly confirms the door/wall positions and 3 m spacing.
  Rooms 2-4 have confirmed trial sequences; their physical measurements
  were not separately logged. Door placement makes a doorway signal path
  possible, including during trials with the door closed.
- The original height target was 103 cm from the floor to the lowest point
  of each board, excluding the cable. Room 1's field report gives 100 cm
  for both boards. Use 100 cm for Room 1; leave Rooms 2-4 unconfirmed.
- Across the five candidate classrooms, three had closed windows and two
  had open windows. Room 1 was confirmed closed. Window states for Rooms
  2-4 and the room-by-room mapping of wooden/metallic whiteboards are
  unrecorded. Benches were described as about 80 cm high. These are site
  notes, not measured per-room labels.

Room layouts and participants
Each classroom had a different layout. For trials with someone inside,
Room 1 used person A, Rooms 2 and 3 used person B, and Room 4 used person C.
Empty trials had no indoor participant. The letters refer only to the
indoor volunteer; corridor passersby were recorded as a separate condition.
Rooms 2 and 3 allow a comparison across layouts with the same volunteer.
For Rooms 1 and 4, holding out the room also holds out its volunteer. The
data cannot isolate the effects of layout, participant or recording session.

What the activity labels mean
Seated and standing trials had one person inside. The instructions called
for staying still at a marked spot; the proposed chair depth was about 2 m
inside, but the actual chair positions were not logged. Parallel means
walking along the test wall, about 1 m inside. Perpendicular means walking
toward and away from that wall, between the far end of the room and a point
about 1 m inside. Mixed means following one fixed route with both segments.
Room 1 reported these paths directly; Rooms 2-4 followed the named activity
sequence, with no separate endpoint or path-length measurements supplied.

Corridor conditions and repeats
- The protocol called for an empty corridor. Two exceptions were reported.
  Room 1 slot 1: people passed while one person sat inside. Slot 2 was a
  fresh seated recording with the corridor empty. A second BOOT press during
  an active trial is ignored by the firmware; seven trials were saved.
- Room 2 slot 4: one person walked in the corridor while the classroom was
  empty. Use slot 0 for the quiet empty baseline and retain slot 4 to assess
  sensitivity to outside movement.
- Rooms 3 and 4 followed the eight-slot sequence above. Slots 0 and 4 are
  the planned quiet empty repeats; no corridor exception was reported.
  Room 1 has the only standing and mixed-path recordings in this collection.

How the files were produced and how to read them
TX starts transmitting when powered. A BOOT press on RX starts a 5-second
countdown and a 30-second standalone capture. The blue LED stays on through
the countdown and recording, then goes off after saving. The downloader
checked the raw SHA-256 checksum and decoded frame count before clearing
each RX slot. Slot numbers restart for each room; use room plus slot as the
trial identifier.

Open room N / slot... / rx_... / csi.csv for the measurements. Each row is
one received packet. iq_hex holds 128 signed CSI bytes (64 complex pairs)
encoded in hexadecimal. The CSV also gives elapsed time, sequence number,
RSSI in dBm, and channel. wire.bin is the original binary capture; run.json
contains the recording ID, checksum, frame count and downloader label.
The downloader uses "other" for seated and standing. Use the slot map or
folder name for the activity. Room 1 also has a trials.csv with field labels.

Checks and use in analysis
All 31 metadata records report complete; their frame counts match the CSVs.
There are 45,344 frames, all on channel 6 with 128 CSI bytes per frame.
Counts per trial range from 1,314 to 1,499. Use timestamps for time windows
and sequence numbers to check gaps. For occupancy labels, 7 trials are
empty and 24 have one person inside, including the two starred trials with
their respective indoor labels. For quiet-corridor comparisons, set aside
both starred trials. Keep all windows from one trial together when splitting
data; hold out whole classrooms to test performance in a new room.
